Notes :
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
K-Sensitivity Experiment #1

Corpus:
    Small TCP/UDP/HTTP/DNS corpus

K values:
    1, 2, 3, 4, 5

Observation:
    Increasing K consistently decreases Precision@K.

    Recall improves from K=1 to K=2 for both retrievers,
    after which it plateaus.

    rag_chunker_exp:
        Recall reaches 1.0 at K=2 and remains there.

    rag_demo:
        Recall reaches 0.875 at K=2 and does not improve
        even when retrieving the entire available corpus.

    MRR remains 1.0 for all K because the first retrieved
    result is relevant for every query.

Initial conclusion:
    K=2 provides the best precision/recall trade-off for
    this dataset.

Architectural observation:
    Increasing K cannot compensate indefinitely for poor
    knowledge representation or retrieval quality.

=============================================================================
So we have to choose the optimal K as per the business objective

                    Business Objective
                           │
            ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
            ▼              ▼              ▼
        Quality         Latency          Cost
        Precision        Search          Tokens
        Recall            Time            LLM
        MRR
            │              │              │
            └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                           ▼
                    Choose optimal K


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------


For example:

Customer-support RAG:
---------------------

You may prioritize recall:

"Missing the correct troubleshooting procedure is worse than providing a little extra context."

So a larger K may be justified.

Real-time network-management RAG:
--------------------------------

You may prioritize latency + precision:

"Give the engineer the most relevant diagnostic information quickly."

Then a smaller K may be preferable.

Cost-sensitive enterprise assistant
-----------------------------------

You may have:

K ↑
→ more retrieved context
→ larger prompt
→ potentially higher LLM cost
→ potentially higher latency

So the architectural question becomes:

What is the smallest K that satisfies the required business-level retrieval quality?

That is a much better formulation than:

"What K gives the highest recall?"

And this connects directly to the principle you mentioned earlier:

An architect should optimize the system according to business logic, not simply maximize a technical metric.

The flow should be like this :
------------------------------

Business requirement
        ↓
Define acceptable quality
        ↓
Experiment with K
        ↓
Measure Precision / Recall / MRR
        ↓
Measure latency / context / cost
        ↓
Find acceptable trade-off
        ↓
Select K
        ↓
Validate in production


There is no universally optimal K. The optimum K is business-scenario dependent and must be determined experimentally by balancing retrieval quality, latency, context size, and cost against the business objective.
